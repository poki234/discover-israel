Welcome

A Country the Size of New Jersey, Packed with Worlds

Israel compresses deserts, mountains, forests, ancient cities and 270 km of Mediterranean coastline into one small, intense country. Three seas touch its shores, four climate zones shape its landscapes, and over 200 museums fill its cities. You can ski on the Golan in the morning and float in the Dead Sea by lunch.

Beyond the Capital of Cool

Eight Destinations You Can't Skip

Most fly into Tel Aviv, but the country rewards travelers who venture out. Every destination below is a day trip or an easy overnight from the city.

🕌

Jerusalem

The Old City's four quarters pack the Western Wall, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre and the Dome of the Rock into one square kilometre. Walk the ramparts, then get lost in the souk.

1 hr from TLV
🧂

Dead Sea

Earth's lowest point at 430 m below sea level. Float effortlessly in water ten times saltier than the ocean, then coat yourself in mineral mud at Ein Gedi or Ein Bokek beaches.

1.5 hrs from TLV
🏰

Masada

Herod's cliff-top fortress above the Dead Sea. Hike the Snake Path at sunrise or take the cable car; the ruins and the views over the Judean desert are unforgettable.

The White City

Tel Aviv: The Deep Dive

A UNESCO-listed Bauhaus gem wrapped around beaches, tech towers and the craziest food scene in the Middle East. This is a city of neighborhoods — each with its own accent, smell and soundtrack.

Old Jaffa (Yafo)

Tel Aviv's 4,000-year-old soul. Wander the sandstone alleyways of the old port at dusk, watch fishermen on the wharf, browse the galleries and the flea market, and eat hummus at Abu Hassan — arguably the country's most obsessive queue. The hilltop park gives you the postcard skyline shot back at the modern city.

Neve Tzedek

The city's first neighborhood (1887) — now its prettiest. Pastel low-rise houses, boutique fashion, the striking Rohr House architecture, and the Suzanne Dellal dance centre. Come Sunday morning for quiet streets and the best specialty coffee density in the city.

Florentin

The gritty-south-turned-cool quarter. Street art covers entire blocks (take a graffiti tour), specialty bakers and natural-wine bars fill former workshops, and Levinsky Market at its edge is the spice-and-deli heartland of the city.

Rothschild Boulevard

The White City's showcase: a tree-lined central promenade under Bauhaus and Eclectic buildings, espresso kiosks, and startup offices humming above. Visit the Independence Hall where Israel was declared a state in 1948, then join the evening paseo.

Dizengoff & the North

Dizengoff Square (recently beautifully restored) anchors the classic TLV café strip. Continue north to Port Tel Aviv for sunset drinks on the pier, and the Yarkon Park riverside for rowboats and concerts.

Carmel Market (Shuk HaCarmel)

The city's roar: a half-mile of fruit pyramids, halva mountains, bourekas stands, cold-squeezed juice, and hip bakeries squeezing between the stalls. Go Friday morning for the full pre-Shabbat theatre — and try the sabich at the famous corner stands.

🏛️ Tel Aviv Museum of Art

Israeli and modern European masters in a sculptural Herta & Paul Amir building. Free-ish entry days vary; the Impressionist wing punches far above its weight.

🏗️ The Bauhaus Walk

Over 4,000 Bauhaus and International Style buildings — the world's largest concentration. The visitor centre on Bialik Street runs self-guided route maps of the White City.

🎤 Culture at Night

The Israeli Opera, Habima theatre, and Yitzhak Rabin Square's open-air events. Then join the locals on Rothschild or in Florentin's bar alleys until very, very late.

14 km of Coastline

The Beach Strip, Beach by Beach

The whole city faces the Mediterranean. Lifeguarded (swim only between the flags!), with a continuous promenade — you can walk or rent a bike from Jaffa to the northern port.

🏖️ Gordon & Frishman

The classic scene: beach volleyball nets, matkot (paddleball) clacking, and the famous deckchair-and-mattress service in summer.

🐕 Hilton Beach

The north end's free spirit — a dog beach on one side, a surf break with real waves on the other. Sunset drum circles are a tradition.

🧘 Metzitzim

Family-friendly and calmer, with the legendary Metzitzim beach bar next door. Great for morning swims before work.

🌅 Banana Beach

By Old Jaffa — the backpacker classic, bonfires at night, and the best angle to photograph Jaffa's ridge at golden hour.

Eat Like a Local

The Food Pilgrimages

Tel Aviv is a top-tier food city. These are the plates that define it — and where locals actually queue for them.

🥙

Hummus

Abu Hassan (Jaffa) — creamy, lemony, served with warm pita and ful. Hummus Abu Dabi and Hummus Felafel on Yehuda Halevi hold their own cult followings.

🍳

Shakshuka

Eggs poached in tomato-pepper stew. Dr. Shakshuka in Jaffa serves it in the pan; every café has a version — order extra bread, always.

🫓

Sabich

Fried eggplant, boiled egg, tahini and amba (pickled mango) in pita — the Iraqi-Jewish gift to breakfast. The Carmel Market corner stands are the temple.

🥖

Bourekas

Flaky Balkan pastries stuffed with cheese, potato or mushrooms. Rule of locals: sprinkle with sesame, dip in pickles brine, eat standing up.

🐟

Markets & Meydan

Sarona Market for the modern food-hall crawl; Levinsky for Persian spice houses and aged cheese; Jaffa's flea market for hip bistros in courtyards.

🍺

Arak & Wine Nights

Natural-wine bars across Florentin and Neve Tzedek, Israeli craft breweries, and arak with grapefruit juice on any beach bar's chalkboard.

Perfect First Trip

A 5-Day Master Plan

Built for a first visit: city, coast, ancients, and desert — without ever changing hotels more than once.

Arrival + Jaffa sunset

Land, drop bags, coffee on Rothschild, then Old Jaffa for the flea market, Abu Hassan hummus, and sunset over the skyline from the hilltop park. Dinner in the flea-market courtyards.

The White City

Bauhaus walk in the morning, Bialik Street and the museum, lunch at the Carmel Market, afternoon on Gordon Beach, evening drinks on the Tel Aviv Port pier.

Jerusalem day trip

First train or bus out. Old City: Western Wall, the Cardo, the four quarters, Mahane Yehuda market for lunch, and Mount of Olives views. Return by night for Florentin bars.

Dead Sea & Masada

Sunrise (or cable car) up Masada, Ein Gedi springs hike, then float in the Dead Sea and mud up at Ein Bokek. Home for a well-earned beach-bar dinner.

North coast finale

Caesarea's Roman port and amphitheatre, Haifa's Bahá'í Gardens, and Akko's Crusader halls with its famous hummus. Sunset flight out of Ben Gurion, or one more night in the city that never stops.

Know Before You Go

Practical Intelligence

The stuff guidebooks bury and locals know by heart.

📅 When to Come

March–May and September–November are perfect: warm sea, clear skies, thin crowds. Summer is hot and busy; winter is mild but rainy (16–20°C).

🚆 Getting Around

Trains link the airport to TLV in 20 minutes. Buses + the new light rail cover the city with the Rav-Kav card; electric bikes and scooters everywhere. Shabbat (Fri eve–Sat) pauses most buses.

🗣️ Survival Phrases

Shalom (hello/peace), slicha (excuse me), bevakasha (please), toda (thanks), beseder (it's all good — the national life philosophy).

💸 Money

Shekel (₪/ILS). Cards work almost everywhere; get small cash for shuk stalls. Tipping: 10–12% in restaurants. Tax-free shopping refunds at the airport.

🕐 The Rhythm

Shops closed Friday evening + Saturday for Shabbat (restaurants and beaches open). Sunday is a workday. Dinner reservations genuinely needed Thu–Sat.

🔒 Safety Sense

Tourist areas are well-policed; normal city precautions apply. Follow Home Front Command alerts if sirens sound; drink water and use sunscreen like it's your job.

🔌 Plug In

Type C/H sockets, 230V — European plugs fit. Local SIMs from Partner/HOT/Pelephone at the airport, or just use the free beach and café Wi-Fi.

🛍️ Bring Home

Dead Sea skincare, halva and za'atar from the shuk, Jaffa flea-market finds, and olive wood from Nazareth. Wine from the Golan is underrated.

THE BIG BEAN HAS AWOKEN

Long before Jaffa had a name, before Rothschild had a boulevard, there was The Big Bean — a legume of impossible size and unfathomable purpose.

They say he patrols the Carmel Market at 3 a.m., tasting nothing, judging everything. They say his shadow once covered the whole of Dizengoff Square, and that the street artists of Florentin paint him from memory — never from life, for those who paint him from life are never seen again.

And they say — in whispers, over hummus, in the darkest corner of Abu Hassan — that he carries daggers. Not for violence. Never for violence. The daggers are ceremonial: twin blades of ancient chickpea-bronze, used only to open the perfect pita and to cut the pretension out of any conversation. Witness accounts describe the hilts: one carved from an olive branch, one from a Rothschild bench he personally requisitioned.

You found all seven of his scattered forms. He acknowledges this. He considers you beseder.

"Fear the Bean. Respect the Bean. And never, ever, order the hummus cold." — Florentin street art, attributed
